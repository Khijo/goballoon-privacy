1. Who we are

Go, Balloon! ("the game") is made by Marcos Oliver (BeBetter), an independent developer in Spain (European Union). BeBetter is the name he publishes his games under; it is not a company. He decides how the data described here is used (he is the "data controller" under the GDPR).

Contact: bebetter.mo@gmail.com

2. In short

  • The game has no accounts. It never asks for your name, email, phone number, photos or contacts.
  • Your progress, settings, coins and balloons are stored only on your device.
  • Anonymous statistics are sent only if you switch them on. They are off by default.
  • The game is free and shows ads from Google AdMob. Google collects some data to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud. Where the law requires it (European Economic Area, United Kingdom, Switzerland), Google's consent form asks you first, and ads are personalized only if you agree.
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play. We never see your payment details.

3. Data stored on your device

To work, the game saves on your device:

  • your progress: best score, number of runs, daily mission progress and streak;
  • your coins, the balloons you own, the balloon you use and whether you bought "Remove ads";
  • your settings: music and sound volume, vibration on or off, and your privacy choices: "Share anonymous statistics" (on the "Before you play" screen and in Settings) and your answer to Google's consent form;
  • counters used to space out the ads between runs (for example, when the last one was shown);
  • a random install ID (see section 4);
  • only while statistics are on: the statistics waiting to be sent (for example when you have no internet connection), at most a few hundred events. They are deleted once sent, and all of them are deleted when you switch statistics off.

Google's consent tool also stores your consent choices on your device, in the standard format of the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework, so that the ads can respect them.

This data stays on your device: we don't receive it. It is deleted when you clear the game's data or uninstall the game.

The game also reads your device language to show the game in English or Spanish. It is not used for anything else, except as described in section 4 if you switch statistics on.

Test versions only. Before a release, a few testers use a test version of the game ("Go, Balloon! (Test)"), which is not on the store. It has one more switch, "Save my runs" (off by default). When it is on, the test version also saves on the device a summary of each run and of what happened in it (for example date and time, height, score, coins, hits and how the run ended), with the install ID, the version number, the phone model (never the name you gave your phone) and how smoothly the game ran. These summaries leave the device only when the tester presses "Send my runs" (or "Copy") and sends them, with an app the tester chooses (for example WhatsApp or email), to the developer, who keeps them with the tester's name in the game's test reports. "Delete my runs" deletes them from the device.

4. Anonymous statistics (optional)

If you switch on "Share anonymous statistics" (on the "Before you play" screen shown the first time you open the game, or later in Settings), the game sends anonymous information about how it is played. We use it only to improve the game, for example to make it neither too easy nor too hard.

What is sent:

  • game events: the game was opened (with the game language and whether it is the first time); a run started; a run ended (altitude, score, coins, duration, highest environment reached, what ended it, whether you continued after an ad); a life was restored; the astronaut helmet appeared; an offer to continue after an ad and its result; whether an ad between runs was shown; the shop was opened; a purchase (which item and the result, never payment details) or a restore of purchases (the result); a balloon was equipped; the daily mission was completed or the streak was lost; a setting was changed; the credits were opened;
  • with every event: which kind of version of the game sent it (the store version or a test version) and its version number, the time of the event, and a random event number used only to avoid counting the same event twice;
  • a random install ID, created on your device the first time the game runs. It is used only to count sessions and the days you come back. It is not linked to your name, email, device name, location or advertising ID. It changes if you clear the game's data.

What is never sent with the statistics: your name, email, phone number, contacts, location, device name or model, advertising ID or any other device identifier.

You can switch statistics off at any time in Settings. From then on nothing is sent, and the statistics still waiting on your device are deleted.

The statistics are processed for us by PostHog Inc. in its PostHog Cloud EU service, hosted on servers in Frankfurt, Germany (PostHog: https://posthog.com/privacy). PostHog processes the data only on our behalf (it is a "data processor"), under the data processing agreement we have signed with it. The statistics are set up with no user profiles, no automatic collection (only the events listed above) and no location lookup, and the project is set to discard the IP address that reaches PostHog with each request (like any internet service, PostHog receives it when the data is sent). The data travels encrypted (HTTPS).

5. Ads

The game is free thanks to ads:

  • an optional ad that you can choose to watch to keep playing after losing your last life (once per run);
  • short ads between runs, never during a run. Buying "Remove ads" turns these off.

Ads are provided by Google AdMob (Google; Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy, and how Google uses information from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). For AdMob, Google acts as an independent controller of the data it collects. According to Google's documentation, the Google Mobile Ads software in the game collects and shares with Google, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

  • your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location;
  • interactions such as the app being opened, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information about the performance of the game and of the ads software, such as launch time, hangs and energy use;
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID, the app set ID and, where applicable, other identifiers related to the accounts signed in on the device.

The data travels encrypted (TLS). The ads software also declares permissions for Android's "Privacy Sandbox" advertising features (ad ID, ad measurement and ad topics); Google has announced that these features are being phased out, and the game itself doesn't use them.

Your choice about ads. Where the law requires consent (European Economic Area, United Kingdom, Switzerland), Google's consent form (Google's "User Messaging Platform", a consent tool certified for the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework) is shown when you open the game, before any ad is requested, and asks whether Google and its partners may store or read information on your device and show you personalized ads. If you refuse, you still see ads, but they are not personalized. Google explains that non-personalized ads are not based on your past behaviour but on the context (such as the app and your general location), and still use identifiers to limit how often you see an ad and for aggregated reporting where your choices allow it; when your choices don't allow that, Google may show "limited ads", which use no personal data for personalization and use local storage only to detect fraud and abuse. You can change your choice at any time with "Privacy options" in the game's Settings (shown where Google's form applies).

You can also reset or delete your device's advertising ID in your device settings (on Android: Settings > Privacy > Ads; the names can vary by phone) and, when you are signed in to your Google Account, choose whether Google personalizes ads on its services and on partner apps at https://myadcenter.google.com.

6. Purchases

You can buy optional items with real money: three premium balloons and "Remove ads". Purchases are processed by Google Play, under Google's privacy policy. We never receive your payment details. The game receives from Google Play only the confirmation of what you bought, so it can unlock it, and stores it on your device. "Restore purchases" asks Google Play again which items you own.

If statistics are on, the game also records an anonymous event with the item and the result (section 4).

7. Game engine

The game is made with the Unity engine (Unity Technologies). The game uses none of Unity's data services: no Unity Analytics, no Unity Ads, no crash reporting, and the engine's diagnostics are switched off. The engine itself may still send Unity Technologies technical information about the device and the game (hardware and software statistics) to produce aggregated statistics. Unity explains its own processing in its privacy policy: https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy.

9. Who receives data

We don't sell your data. Data leaves your device only to:

  • Google, for ads and for the consent form (section 5), as an independent controller;
  • PostHog Inc., only if you switched statistics on (section 4), as our processor;
  • Google Play, for purchases (section 6);
  • in test versions only, the developer, when a tester sends their runs with the app they choose (section 3).

Google may process ads data outside the European Union, for example in the United States. Google LLC is certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and uses standard contractual clauses where they are needed (https://policies.google.com/privacy/frameworks).

PostHog stores the statistics on its servers in the EU (Frankfurt, Germany). PostHog Inc. is a company based in the United States and uses other service providers (its "subprocessors": https://posthog.com/subprocessors); if any data is transferred outside the EU, PostHog's data processing agreement covers it with the EU standard contractual clauses.

10. How long data is kept

  • Data on your device: until you clear the game's data or uninstall the game.
  • Statistics: up to 1 year. PostHog keeps our events for the period our plan sets (1 year) and doesn't let us see or use older ones; its documentation doesn't give a date on which older events are permanently erased from its systems. Statistics waiting on your device are deleted once sent or when you switch statistics off.
  • Ads data: as set by Google (https://policies.google.com/technologies/retention).
  • Messages you send us: as long as needed to answer you, and then deleted.
  • Runs that testers send (test versions only): with the game's test reports while the game is being developed; a tester can ask us to delete them at any time.

11. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict its use, and to receive it in a portable format. You can object to uses based on legitimate interest, and you can withdraw your consent at any time (this doesn't affect what was done before). To use these rights, write to bebetter.mo@gmail.com. We will answer within one month.

The statistics are linked only to a random install ID, not to your name or your device. The game doesn't show you this ID, so we may not be able to tell which statistics are yours; if we can't, we will tell you in our answer. In any case: switching statistics off stops new data from being sent and deletes the statistics still waiting on your device, clearing the game's data creates a new ID, and the statistics are used for up to 1 year (section 10).

For the data collected by Google for ads, you can change your choices with "Privacy options" in Settings, reset or delete your advertising ID, and use Google's own tools (https://myadcenter.google.com and https://policies.google.com/privacy).

You can also complain to the Spanish data protection authority (Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, www.aepd.es) or to the authority of your country.

12. Children

The game is for a general audience aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them; ad requests are not marked as directed at children. In the European Union, the age from which you can give consent to online services yourself is set by each country, between 13 and 16 (14 in Spain). If you are younger than that age in your country, ask a parent or guardian before switching statistics on or accepting personalized ads in Google's form. If you think a child has given us data, contact us and we will delete what we can.

13. Security

Data sent by the game, by Google's ads software and to PostHog is encrypted in transit (HTTPS / TLS). Data on your device is kept in the game's private storage.

14. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update it at https://khijo.github.io/goballoon-privacy/ and change the date at the top. Important changes will also be mentioned in the store listing, and if a change needs your consent, the game will ask you before it applies.
